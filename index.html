📊 The Economist — Net AI Jobs Impact (2023–2026)

Two charts from The Economist measure the net impact of the AI boom on US employment from 2023 through 2026 by comparing actual job counts to expected baseline hiring trends ("Actual jobs minus jobs expected if sectors followed broader hiring trends").

🧱 Chart 1: Hard-hat labour

This chart displays additional AI-related infrastructure jobs (blue-collar/trades), showing a steady, accelerating rise from 2023 into 2026, reaching around 300,000 net additional jobs.

  • Physical Data Center Boom: Building out the physical infrastructure required to train and run AI models (data centers, power grids, cooling systems) requires physical labor that cannot be automated by software.
  • Key Sectors Driving Growth:
    • Electrical contractors (red) and utility-system construction (blue) make up the largest share, as AI facilities require massive electrical power grid expansions.
    • Commercial construction, HVAC & plumbing (for liquid and air cooling in server farms), and electrical-equipment manufacturing contribute the rest.

💻 Chart 2: STEM with the flow

This chart tracks additional AI-related white-collar jobs, total gain approaching ~600,000–700,000 net jobs by 2026, but with a distinct mid-period dip in specific roles.

  • The Software Developer Dip (2024): Notice how software developers (red) dipped into negative territory throughout 2024 (down to around -100k relative to baseline). Early AI adoption increased code efficiency and enabled tech layoffs, briefly slowing hiring for entry-to-mid-level coders.
  • The Rebound & Expansion (2025–2026): By 2025, software developer hiring recovered back into positive growth alongside major increases in:
    • Maths & data science (orange) and info-security analysts (light blue)
    • Engineers (dark blue)
    • Other computer occupations (grey), which forms the largest overall category as AI integration broadens across non-coding tech roles.

🎯 Core Takeaway

The AI boom isn't just creating digital jobs — it is driving massive demand for physical infrastructure. While software engineering underwent a temporary correction in 2024 as companies adapted to AI tools, both physical infrastructure trades and white-collar STEM fields show strong overall net job creation driven by AI investment.
